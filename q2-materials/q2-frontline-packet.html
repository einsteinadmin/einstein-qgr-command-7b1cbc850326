How the Company Did

Q2 in Plain English

What — Record revenue, profit behind plan

We did $8.4M in revenue — up almost 10% over last year, the biggest quarter in company history! And we didn't just ride a good market: the moving markets around us grew about 2–5% — we grew at roughly 3× the market's pace. We hit ~95% of our revenue plan. Profit came in at about two-thirds of what we planned — record revenue did not mean record profit this quarter. (July numbers are estimates until books close ~Aug 1.)

So What — The yardstick was off, and the spending was on purpose (a lot of it on you)

The revenue miss: here's the thing we found when we dug in — the "miss" mostly wasn't a miss. We actually served more hours of moving than we had budgeted, which means most branches did MORE moves than we expected. The problem was the plan itself: we didn't end up charging the prices we had planned to charge in the budget.

Think of it like a lemonade stand. We planned the summer assuming $2 a cup. We poured more cups than planned — but a lot of them sold at the old $1.75 price, so the cash box came up short of plan even while we sold more lemonade than ever. That's mostly a planning miss, and Paul and I own it.

The profit gap: mostly us choosing to invest more money into the company than expected, plus more overtime than we budgeted and a few pop-up costs we didn't see coming (like a $30K plumbing repair at our North Dallas location).

The biggest choice was you: in mid-May we gave every mover a raise, about $1.85/hour on average. That wasn't budgeted in the quarter. We moved it up because it was the right call, not because the spreadsheet had room — and we'd make the same call again.

The rest of the gap was more bets on growth:

  • Summer crews — built early, and kept when May started slow
  • Leadership coaching and a stronger finance team
  • A bigger marketing push
  • 17 trucks — 4 net-new, 13 replacements

Meanwhile the company stayed strong where it counts: revenue covered our bills roughly 1.5 times over. Nobody should read this quarter as "the company is in trouble." It isn't.

Now What — More crews on trucks, faster follow-up on leads

We proved we can win demand — leads nearly doubled and we out-grew the market. Q3 points at two things. First, getting more crews on trucks: we finished the quarter with more work available than crews to run it (more on that two cards down). Second, turning leads into booked jobs faster — for CERs and sales, speed-to-lead is the play. For movers: keep availability up, keep NPS up, and send us people you'd want on your own crew. Your branch's specific picture is on your scorecard, linked at the bottom.

What — Two honest ways to count a truck

Count every truck on the lot and we ran ~76% utilization — that's the number we grade ourselves on, and we beat the target. Count only the trucks we actually had crews for, and we ran ~84% — on that basis, 9 of 10 branches beat their target. The difference between those two numbers is 861 truck-days — 861 separate days where a truck sat parked from open to close because there was no crew to run it. Real work left on the table.

So What — The crews performed; we needed more crews

This means the movers we did hire were utilized well and got the hours we intended — plus some, since we ran way more overtime than we budgeted this quarter. We just needed to hire even more crews to open up even more trucks. We hired to ~101% of the staffing plan and it still wasn't enough — the plan was set below what summer demand actually needed, by roughly 40 movers company-wide. That's a target-setting miss on the planning side, not a hustle problem on yours.

Now What — Full crews turn parked trucks into paychecks

Q3 staffing targets are re-set to real demand — which means the hours are there, and promotion slots open as crews grow. Two asks: keep your availability up, and if you know somebody who works the way you do, refer them. A parked truck with a full crew becomes hours, tips, and a bigger branch.

What

The new quote form nearly doubled our leads (+97%) and the share of visitors starting a quote more than doubled (13% → 29%). Booyah.

So What

We used to lose ~6 of 10 people who got an estimate — now we keep every one as a contactable lead. More leads = more booked jobs = more hours and bigger branches.

The honest flip side: this worked almost too well. The flood of new leads made it harder to get back to customers as fast as we expect. And a lot of these are "lower intent" leads — people who would have abandoned the cart before ever giving us their contact info on the old form. Those two things combined to lower our overall conversion rate.

Now What

Q3 is about two things with those leads: working them fast and working the right ones first (lead triage). Honest answer on how 4,300+ leads went unworked: the old form only produced leads who asked to be contacted. The new one captures everyone — and nobody yet owned that new queue or had a speed target for it. Now it's getting owned: the quote form's been updated, the mobile app is next, and speed-to-lead is becoming the sales team's headline number.

What

Our fleet safety score climbed to 89, up from 80 last quarter, and the share of drivers flagged high-risk nearly halved. Following Distance events fell 54% — the quarter's biggest safety win. Total events rose only because we deliberately tightened two standards (Rolling Stop, Inattentive) — that's stricter refereeing, not worse driving.

And here's the number that hits the wallet: just 3 insurance claims in all of 2026 so far, roughly $15K out of pocket — versus 54 claims in all of 2025.

So What

Fewer real events = fewer claims = money that stays in the company and the bonus pool instead of going to the insurance company. And this one's bigger than a bonus line: over the last couple of years our insurance rates rose so fast it was becoming one of the biggest existential threats to the business. Going from 54 claims to 3 is how that threat gets beat — safe driving is one of the most direct ways a crew protects its own branch, and the whole company.

Now What

Two fixes are live: iPad-looking Inattentive alerts can't be dismissed anymore, and San Antonio's broken visor gets fixed, not dismissed.

The Q1 Scoreboard — You Talked. Here's What Happened.

After April's mover survey, leadership made six commitments in direct response to what movers asked for. Here's where each one stands, three months later:

  • ✔ DONE — Mover comp review. Base pay went up across every branch in May. The #1 ask on the survey, answered with real money.
  • ✔ BUILT — Performance-based lead promotions. The framework is designed and with HR now: earn the next rung on performance, reviewed every pay period — not tenure, not who-you-know.
  • ✔ LIVE — Call grading on sales calls. Automated grading now runs on real sales calls, feeding coaching on estimate accuracy — the #1 frustration movers named. The training side keeps deepening this quarter.
  • ✔ LIVE — Digital training handbook. The Mover Training App is live across all of DFWT — three weeks in with zero bugs — with SOPs and training content building into it. Company-wide rollout is the plan.
  • ✔ LANDS AUG 1 — Hiring for culture interviews. Culture-first interviews replace the old trial-day format starting August 1 — protecting the hiring bar movers told us they value.
  • ✔ DONE — Better mobile tools. EMMA got new mobile views on almost every page — dramatically easier to use from the truck than it was in April.
So What — This survey moves real decisions

Every item on that scoreboard started as a survey answer. This isn't a suggestion box that goes into a drawer — it's how the company decides where to put its money and its energy. Same deal this cycle.

Now What — What you told us in July, and what we're doing about it

The loudest theme in the July survey, by a wide margin: develop people faster. More structured training for managers, clearer career paths, clearer roles. That's exactly where the company is pointed:

  • Branch scorecards & dashboards — every manager sees the same numbers as leadership, so coaching conversations run on facts, not opinions.
  • Consistent check-ins & performance management — a regular check-in rhythm tied to clear expectations, so nobody wonders where they stand or what earns the next step.
  • The Mover Training App — develops movers faster today, and the same platform carries driver and lead training next. One place to learn the job and see your progress.
  • This quarter's training theme — squarely aimed at developing movers and managers quicker (named in the Q3 Theme card below).
  • Real career ladders — HR is defining what Branch Manager, Ops Manager, and Field Manager each own, so the rungs are distinct and the path up is visible — paired with the performance-based promotion framework.

And the survey itself is changing: this is the last cycle where participation is mandatory. From here, the plan is to collect this feedback more regularly — movers and managers alike — instead of one big survey every six months. Smaller asks, more often, so problems surface in weeks instead of quarters. When one lands, answer it: the scoreboard above is what your answers turn into.
